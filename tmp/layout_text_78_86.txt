4.4    Physics-Guided Feature Engineering
Before the engineered feature architecture was finalized, an early nine-feature MLP
trained using direct pressure and diameter inputs revealed a structural problem. Nozzle
diameter exists at only six levels in the dataset, and these are not uniformly available
across all pressure combinations: all six diameter levels appear exclusively within the
family (𝑃inj = 2000 bar, 𝑃cb = 4, 𝑃ch ∈ {5, 10, 15} bar), whereas injection pressure
has only four levels. Figure 24 makes this discretely sampled grid explicit: most
cells in the (chamber pressure, injection pressure, diameter) grid contain zero or one
observation, and full six-diameter coverage exists only along a single pressure family.
    Because a smooth MLP treats these as continuous inputs, it generates derivative
structures along the diameter and pressure axes that the data does not constrain.
Figure 25 shows the result of dense diameter sweeps at 𝑡 = 0.85 ms: between adjacent
observation levels, the predicted mean curve undergoes five to six gradient sign
reversals per slice, and autodiff gradients peak at ∼ 4 × 104 over a support span of
about 0.05 mm, equating to a curvature norm on the order of 107 . The same diagnostic
on injection pressure showed non-monotonic, unsupported extrapolation over sparsely
supported slices. The root issue is structural. A smooth MLP is asked to learn
continuous laws from what is effectively a discrete grid, so adding regularization
can only suppress the instability at the cost of useful capacity. Therefore, the revised
architecture addresses the problem at the source.
    The revised surrogate model first replaces the weakly supported pressure and
diameter inputs with a single scaling group. The Hiroyasu–Arai post-breakup branch
[13] provides the dimensional starting point:

    Stage 1 and Stage 2 also share a group-aware data split: each operating-condition
group, defined jointly by nozzle identity, injection pressure, chamber pressure, and
injection duration, is assigned as a whole to the training, validation, or test set to prevent
leakage between curves that share the same operating point. Without this constraint, a
random trajectory-level split would let the network interpolate between nearly identical
samples drawn from the same operating condition seen during training; the holdout
error would then reflect within-condition smoothness rather than generalization across
the condition space. Therefore, group assignment is not a mere implementation detail
but a structural constraint that ensures the reported test metrics measure interpolation
across different operating conditions, not memorization within a condition. Stage
2 warm-starts from Stage 1’s engineering run directory and reuses its scaler states,
ensuring that variant selection and 𝐴 scaling remain consistent across the two stages.

The construction of Dataset #2 starts by unrolling the individual (𝑡, 𝑆) discrete
observation points from the cleaned CDF wide-format sequence table. Even after
excluding points from trajectories that reached the spatial FOV cap, the remaining
discrete points exhibit condition-dependent late-time observability loss: although a
spray plume can remain within the field of view, the Mie scattering signal may fall
below the detection threshold because of droplet breakup, spatial dilution, and local
drops in droplet number density. Some conditions enter this signal-loss regime at
𝑡 < 0.5 ms, while others survive until significantly later. To address this, a point
table generation tool was constructed (codebase identifiers listed in Appendix A) to
determine condition-level truncation thresholds for each of the 592 operating condition
groups using two statistical/optical metrics: (i) the smoothed trajectory count per
bin drops below a fixed fraction of its intra-condition peak (density-drop signal-loss
criterion), or (ii) the per-bin 𝑝 95 penetration reaches 95% of the estimated FOV cap
(FOV-cap criterion). Figure 27 illustrates one representative example of each. The
first bin satisfying either criterion, along with all subsequent observation points, is
discarded, leaving 𝑁unc = 506 634 censor-truncated points for downstream log-log
regression use.

lose detectable Mie support. After truncating each condition at its selected threshold,
