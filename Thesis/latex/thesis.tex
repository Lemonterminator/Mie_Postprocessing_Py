%%%%%%% Float placement %%%%%%%
% aaltothesis.cls sets topfraction=0.9 and textfraction=0.1, which permits a
% float occupying almost the whole page to sit at the TOP of a text page with as
% little as 10% body text below it.  When the surrounding paragraph -- or an
% itemize list -- runs across that page break, the reader sees it visually cut in
% half by the figure.  \textfraction is the effective lever: it is the minimum
% share of a mixed page that must be body text, so 0.18 forces a genuine block of
% text onto any page that also carries a float, and pushes oversized floats onto
% a float page of their own instead.
%
% \floatpagefraction is deliberately left at the class value.  Raising it makes
% float pages harder to emit, which stalls the [p] figures (e.g. the
% 0.88\textheight teacher-distribution plot in Chapter 4); since floats are
% placed FIFO, one unplaceable float defers every float queued behind it and
% whole clusters drift a dozen pages past their reference.  For the same reason
% the floats that are taller than \topfraction carry [tp] rather than [t] in the
% section sources -- a t-only float that no longer fits at the top can never be
% placed at all, and blocks the queue until the next \clearpage.
\renewcommand{\topfraction}{0.75}
\renewcommand{\bottomfraction}{0.3}
\renewcommand{\textfraction}{0.18}
% With 111 floats over ~180 pages the default budget of 2 top / 3 total floats
% per page cannot drain the queue fast enough; raise it so deferred floats catch
% up close to their reference instead of accumulating.
\setcounter{topnumber}{3}
\setcounter{totalnumber}{5}

\setmainabstract{Pilot diesel injections in multi-fuel marine engines are short, transient events. If the liquid spray reaches the piston crown or cylinder wall before ignition, it can cause wall wetting, lubricant dilution, and poor mixture preparation. This thesis develops a workflow converting high-speed Mie-scattering spray videos into penetration measurements and surrogate models for early spray--wall impingement risk assessment, using room-temperature, non-reacting recordings from the W\"artsil\"a Optical Spray Combustion Chamber across multiple operating conditions and nozzle geometries.

The workflow combines multi-hole video processing, plume-wise feature extraction, and a reduced-order trajectory fit smoothing noisy labels and mitigating right-censoring bias at the field-of-view limit. Two central contributions are censoring-aware measurement-to-target construction and empirical scaling analysis: on the Level-3 quality-controlled archive, the effective pressure-difference exponent ranges from approximately 0.45 to 0.69 over 0.3--0.7 ms, moving towards the classical 0.25 post-breakup value as sample support decreases. These results motivate, but do not elevate to a universal spray law, the physics-guided scaling used by the surrogate.

These targets feed a three-stage curriculum predicting penetration mean and heteroscedastic uncertainty. On 540,367 points from 9 nozzle campaigns under uncensored evaluation, the final multilayer perceptron (MLP) model achieves a five-seed mean root mean squared error (RMSE) of 4.19 mm and mean absolute error (MAE) of 2.98 mm with conservative $1\sigma$/$2\sigma$ coverage of 88.8\%/99.0\%, reducing RMSE by more than 54\% relative to calibrated Hiroyasu--Arai and Naber--Siebers baselines. Post-training feature-wise linear modulation (FiLM) adaptation mainly improved calibration over a pooled baseline, while a sparse variational Gaussian process (SVGP) alternative attains better point accuracy but markedly less conservative coverage. A simplified geometric projection maps predicted penetration onto wall-impingement screening scores; the workflow is an early-stage screening tool, ahead of---not in place of---computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulation and engine testing.}
